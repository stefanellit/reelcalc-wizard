Why the Fluorocarbon Was Replaced

The Curado started with 65 yards of InvizX over mono backing. After reties, trimming, tangles, and break-offs, that top section had gotten short. There had also been two breaks during casts that seemed to happen away from the knot. The cause was unclear.

It was time for fresh line, and a chance to try mono for squarebill fishing. That does not mean InvizX was faulty; it was simply a different line to try on this setup.

Why This 10 lb Mono Was Chosen

The ReelCalc line database helped find a mono close to the thickness of the old InvizX. With a similar diameter and the same length, the new line should take up about the same spool space. The diameter mattered, not just the 10 lb label.

The ReelCalc Setup

Entering the new Sunline line and a 65-yard length gave 108 yards of Big Game backing, matching the earlier setup. That backing was still usable, so there was no need to start over.

The reel used here is model CU200HGM, matching the Curado 200 M 200 HG RH entry selected in the calculator.

How the Curado Was Re-Spooled

Strip Off the Old Line

The shortened InvizX was removed down to the backing knot.

The Big Game underneath was still usable, so it stayed on the spool. No new backing was installed.

The new Sunline mono was joined to the existing Big Game backing using a Uni-to-Uni knot.

The Sunline spool went on the stand, with the line running through the Rapala counter. The new mono was wound on with steady tension.

Trying This on Your Own Reel?

Is 65 yards enough for everyone?

No. It was the amount chosen for this squarebill setup. Leave enough working line for your longest casts, fish runs, and trimming. Your fishing may call for a longer section.

What if the new line is thicker, or more line is needed?

Check the backing amount again with ReelCalc. Either change takes up more space, so some backing may need to come off. Keep an eye on the fill as you wind; do not force on extra line just to reach a number.

Can backing stay on the reel indefinitely?

No. Reuse it only while it is still in good condition. If it is damaged or questionable, replace it rather than covering it with new line.

A Note About This Re-Spool

This was a refill over existing backing, not a test of how much line an empty Curado can hold. The 108-yard backing figure comes from the earlier ReelCalc setup; it was not remeasured. Only the fresh line went through the counter, and the final fill was checked by eye.

The useful takeaway: Changing line does not always mean stripping the whole reel. With a similar diameter and the same working length, this Curado could switch from fluoro to mono and keep its usable backing.
