Daiwa Tatula LT 2500D-XH Line Capacity & Reel Setup Guide

The original Tatula LT 2500D-XH is a useful size to consider for bass finesse and general freshwater spinning. Its 6.2 oz weight, ZAION frame and listed 34.5-inch retrieve balance light handling with quick line pickup. Daiwa rates it for 210 yards of 10 lb mono. Choose your actual line below to estimate a refill with or without backing; this guide covers TALT2500D-XH, not Tatula MQ.

Quick Answer: Best Line for the Daiwa Tatula LT 2500D-XH

Best overall range: 10-15 lb braid

Simple alternative: 6-8 lb mono/fluoro

10-15 lb braid is ReelCalc's starting suggestion, not a manufacturer limit. A straight-line alternative is 6-8 lb mono/fluoro. Match either choice to your rod and fishing conditions; backing is optional.

Who Is the Daiwa Tatula LT 2500D-XH For?

For bass finesse and general freshwater lure fishing with fast slack pickup, choose your line around the rod, presentation and cover. This guide follows TALT2500D-XH; do not substitute the capacity of a similarly named reel from another generation.

Best Line Setup for the Daiwa Tatula LT 2500D-XH

Start with the line range that suits your rod and fishing. You can fill with main line or use backing beneath a shorter working length. For braid, secure the base against slipping and spool under steady tension; stop at a sensible clearance below the lip.

Daiwa Tatula LT 2500D-XH FAQs

What Size Braid Is Best for the Daiwa Tatula LT 2500D-XH?

10-15 lb braid is the practical ReelCalc range for this reel. Use the lighter end when fishing small lures for trout in open water. The upper end can provide more margin around light cover or when targeting bass, but keep the line matched to the rod and lure so the setup still handles light presentations well.

How Much Line Does the Daiwa Tatula LT 2500D-XH Hold?

The capacity table shows the manufacturer ratings for TALT2500D-XH. Your chosen line may be thicker or thinner than the line behind a pound-test rating. Use its diameter in the calculator and watch the actual fill level rather than trying to force on the last few yards.
