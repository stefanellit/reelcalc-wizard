Daiwa Tatula LT 1000D-XH Line Capacity & Reel Setup Guide

The original Tatula LT 1000D-XH pairs a 5.8 oz reel with a deep spool for light freshwater fishing. Daiwa lists 160 yards of 6 lb mono and a 30.5-inch retrieve, so it offers reserve and quick slack pickup for small-lure work. Its ZAION frame belongs to the original LT design, not the later MQ. Choose your line below for a full-spool estimate or an optional-backing setup.

Quick Answer: Best Line for the Daiwa Tatula LT 1000D-XH

Best overall range: 6-10 lb braid

Simple alternative: 2-6 lb mono/fluoro

6-10 lb braid is ReelCalc's starting suggestion, not a manufacturer limit. A straight-line alternative is 2-6 lb mono/fluoro. Match either choice to your rod and fishing conditions; backing is optional.

Who Is the Daiwa Tatula LT 1000D-XH For?

For light freshwater spinning for panfish, trout and small finesse presentations, choose your line around the rod, presentation and cover. This guide follows TALT1000D-XH; do not substitute the capacity of a similarly named reel from another generation.

Best Line Setup for the Daiwa Tatula LT 1000D-XH

Start with the line range that suits your rod and fishing. You can fill with main line or use backing beneath a shorter working length. For braid, secure the base against slipping and spool under steady tension; stop at a sensible clearance below the lip.

Daiwa Tatula LT 1000D-XH FAQs

What Size Braid Is Best for the Daiwa Tatula LT 1000D-XH?

6-10 lb braid is the practical ReelCalc range for this reel. Use the lighter end when fishing small lures for trout and panfish in open water. The upper end can provide more margin around light cover, but keep the line matched to the rod and lure so the setup still handles light presentations well.

How Much Line Does the Daiwa Tatula LT 1000D-XH Hold?

The capacity table shows the manufacturer ratings for TALT1000D-XH. Your chosen line may be thicker or thinner than the line behind a pound-test rating. Use its diameter in the calculator and watch the actual fill level rather than trying to force on the last few yards.
